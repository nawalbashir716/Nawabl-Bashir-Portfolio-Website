problem: |
  This account was taken over in July, with the objective of improving both top-line growth and advertising efficiency within a relatively constrained ad ecosystem.

  Walmart presents a fundamentally different environment compared to Amazon, with several structural limitations: limited campaign control (no negatives, fewer optimization levers), lower conversion rates due to weaker traffic quality, restricted targeting capabilities, and a less mature algorithm and reporting infrastructure.

  At takeover, the account lacked clear budget prioritization, and spend was distributed inefficiently across products and campaigns without a strong performance-driven structure. The key challenge was to drive aggressive growth while operating within these platform constraints, without compromising efficiency.
solution: |
  Budgets were aggressively shifted toward high-converting SKUs, products with stable inventory levels, and campaigns demonstrating consistent ROAS — ensuring capital was deployed where it could scale profitably, rather than spread thin across the catalog.

  Given the inability to negate or heavily refine targeting, extensive keyword and competitor research was conducted upfront, and targeting was built to be as relevant and conversion-focused as possible from day one, minimizing low-intent traffic through tighter campaign structuring.

  Instead of over-fragmentation, campaigns were structured around clear intent clusters, keeping focus on signal clarity rather than volume and reducing internal competition and inefficient spend overlap.

  Scaling decisions were tightly aligned with inventory — high-velocity SKUs were prioritized for growth while spend was reduced on products with limited stock runway, preventing revenue loss from stockouts and wasted ad spend.

  Ongoing deep-dives into campaign-level efficiency, SKU-level profitability, and spend-vs-contribution analysis ensured fast iteration despite limited platform flexibility.
results: |
  Comparing Q3 (Jul–Sep) performance against Q2 (Apr–Jun): GMV grew by 53.71%, units sold by 36.3%, and orders by 41.67%.

  On the advertising side, ad spend increased 60.8%, ad sales increased 103%, and ROAS improved by 26.4% — despite Walmart's more limited campaign controls compared to Amazon.
